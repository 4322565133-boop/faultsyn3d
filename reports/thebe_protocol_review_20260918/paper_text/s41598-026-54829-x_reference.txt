Abstract

  Accurate fault detection in 3D seismic data requires modeling extended geological

structures across multiple dimensions. Recent CNN-transformer hybrids employ windowed

attention (Swin Transformer) or factorized attention, fragmenting volumetric fault

geometries into constrained local interactions that cannot fully capture continuous fault


                                                         S S
planes. Additionally, models frequently misclassify seismic noise as faults due to limited

labeled data and insufficient noise exposure.
                                                      R E
                                                     P
  We present the first application of unfactorized 3D global attention—where every voxel

                                            IN
attends to all other voxels—integrated with volumetric convolution for seismic fault


                              L E
                            C
segmentation. Building on Mahzad et al. (2026), our hybrid U-Net combines 3D convolution


                         T I
for local features with true global self-attention for complete spatial context. Training


                      A R
employs two-stage learning: self-supervised denoising pretext training across multiple real-

world 3D surveys, then discriminative transfer learning on fault-labeled data using layer-

wise learning rate decay and Unified Focal Loss.

  Validation on Thebe survey data achieved Dice=0.853, IoU=0.744, precision=0.842,

MCC=0.841—statistically significant improvements (p<0.001, Cohen's d=0.54–1.02) of 4.5–

8.1% over state-of-the-art 3D CNN+Swin architecture. On unseen test data, improvements

widened to 9.9–16.7% with superior generalization (3.6% vs 8.3% degradation). Results

establish that unfactorized global attention with noise-aware pretext training significantly

improves fault detection accuracy and generalization.

1 Introduction
                             ACCEPTED
                               ARTICLEMANUSCRIPT
                                       IN PRESS



  Seismic fault detection remains critical yet challenging in subsurface interpretation,

directly impacting hydrocarbon exploration, carbon sequestration, and geothermal

development. Accurate fault network identification is essential for understanding structural

compartmentalization, fluid flow pathways, and sealing capacities governing reservoir

performance. Despite advances in seismic acquisition, fault interpretation remains labor-

intensive, requiring expert geological knowledge and subjective judgment varying among

interpreters. Deep learning has transformed seismic interpretation workflows, yet practical

deployment remains constrained by two fundamental challenges: scarcity of expert-labeled

training data and architectures capable of capturing complex three-dimensional fault

geometries.

  Computational fault detection has evolved over three decades. Early attribute-based



                                                         S S
approaches relied on seismic attributes to highlight structural discontinuities. Coherence



                                                      R E
attributes (Bahorich & Farmer, 1995; Marfurt et al., 1998) enabled automated detection as

                                                     P
zones of low trace-to-trace similarity, while variance attributes quantified local amplitude


                                             IN
variations (Van Bemmel & Pepper, 2000). Curvature-based methods showed second-order


                              L E
spatial derivatives correlate with fault systems (Roberts, 2001; Al-Dossary & Marfurt, 2006;


                           IC
                         T
Chopra & Marfurt, 2007a, 2007b). Texture-based approaches captured local unpredictability



                      A R
through chaos attributes (Randen et al., 2000; Chopra & Marfurt, 2016), while ant-tracking

extracted fault surfaces through iterative trace following (Randen et al., 2001; Pedersen et

al., 2002). However, these methods face limitations: extensive manual tuning, high noise

sensitivity, ambiguity where faults overlap with stratigraphic features, and inability to learn

from labeled examples.

  Machine learning approaches explored unsupervised and supervised paradigms.

Unsupervised clustering on multi-attribute feature spaces (Liu et al., 2018) attempted

automatic segmentation but lack spatial awareness and cannot incorporate expert domain

knowledge. Supervised methods including support vector machines and random forests

(Popovici et al., 2017; Tavakolizadeh & Bagheri, 2022; Ren et al., 2023; Esmaeili et al., 2025)
                             ACCEPTED
                               ARTICLEMANUSCRIPT
                                       IN PRESS



learned fault characteristics from labeled data but lack convolutional operations and

hierarchical spatial processing for modeling complex three-dimensional geometries.

  Convolutional neural networks marked a paradigm shift in seismic interpretation (Mahzad

& Riahi, 2024; Mahzad & Bagheri, 2025), enabling automatic hierarchical feature extraction

from seismic amplitudes. Huang et al. (2017) demonstrated end-to-end learning from raw

volumes, while Xiong et al. (2018) showed learned features outperformed traditional

attributes. The FaultSeg3D benchmark (Wu et al., 2019) established standardized evaluation

and demonstrated three-dimensional CNNs trained on synthetic data could segment faults

in field data through transfer learning. Subsequent work explored transfer learning

strategies (Cunha et al., 2020) and focal loss for class imbalance (Wei et al., 2022).

  However, CNNs face an architectural constraint: receptive fields grow linearly with depth.



                                                         S S
Capturing long-range dependencies such as continuous fault planes extending across



                                                      R E
hundreds of voxels requires stacking many convolutional layers, causing vanishing gradients

                                                     P
and training difficulties. This motivated attention mechanisms enabling direct long-range


                                              IN
dependency modeling by computing relationships between spatial positions simultaneously.


                              L E
Recent CNN-transformer hybrids combine local feature extraction with global context. Wang


                           IC
                         T
et al. (2024) employed Swin Transformer with SimMIM self-supervised pre-training, while Li



                      A R
et al. (2025) introduced FaultVitNet combining Vision Transformers with hybrid attention.

  Despite these advances, current methodologies face three fundamental limitations. First,

attention mechanisms compromise volumetric context through spatial factorization or

windowing. Existing architectures employ windowed attention within small local windows or

factorized attention decomposing three-dimensional operations into axis-wise operations.

These strategies address computational tractability but fragment three-dimensional spatial

relationships characterizing extended geological structures. Fault systems comprise

continuous planar surfaces spanning hundreds of voxels, relay ramps connecting segments

across large extents, and complex network topologies where displacement patterns

propagate throughout volumes. Windowed attention restricts voxels to attending only nearby

neighbors, preventing geological continuity modeling beyond window boundaries, while
                             ACCEPTED
                               ARTICLEMANUSCRIPT
                                       IN PRESS



factorized attention processing dimensions independently cannot capture true volumetric

correlations defining three-dimensional fault geometries. Second, pretext tasks lack domain

specificity and fail to address critical failure modes. Current approaches including SimMIM,

masked autoencoders, and rotation prediction are borrowed from computer vision without

modification. While enabling general feature learning from unlabeled data, they do not

exploit seismic-specific properties. Models frequently misclassify random noise and

acquisition artifacts as geological structures because noise patterns—random amplitude

fluctuations,   coherent   acquisition   footprints,   processing   residuals—often    exhibit

discontinuities superficially resembling faults. Generic pretext tasks like reconstructing

masked regions provide no mechanism for learning explicit discrimination between

stochastic noise and spatially coherent geological features. Third, training paradigms remain



                                                           S S
constrained by synthetic data or single-dataset learning. Most approaches either pre-train



                                                        R E
on synthetic fault models, introducing domain shift when transferring to field data, or train

                                                       P
exclusively on single labeled datasets like the Thebe survey, the primary publicly available


                                             IN
three-dimensional volume with expert-labeled faults. Single-setting training limits exposure


                              L E
to structural diversity, acquisition variations, and noise characteristics varying across


                           IC
                         T
surveys.



                      A R
  This study addresses these limitations through a unified framework integrating two key

innovations. First, we present the first application of unfactorized three-dimensional global

attention for seismic fault segmentation. Building upon our hybrid architecture for seismic

denoising (Mahzad et al., 2026), we combine three-dimensional convolutional processing

with true global attention where every voxel attends to all other voxels within sub-volumes

simultaneously, without windowing or axis-wise factorization. This enables capturing both

local geological features through three-dimensional convolutions and extended fault

structures through complete spatial context modeling, preserving continuous fault planes,

relay ramps, and volumetric fault network topologies that windowed or factorized methods

fragment. Second, we introduce denoising as a domain-specific pretext task directly

addressing noise misclassification. By first training the network to distinguish noise patterns
                              ACCEPTED
                                ARTICLEMANUSCRIPT
                                        IN PRESS



from genuine seismic features across multiple geographically distinct unlabeled surveys, the

model develops explicit noise-discrimination capabilities before learning fault-specific

characteristics through supervised fine-tuning. Our two-stage paradigm leverages our three-

dimensional global attention denoising model (Mahzad et al., 2026) trained on real seismic

surveys from the Taranaki Basin, followed by discriminative transfer learning employing

layer-wise learning rate decay, gradual unfreezing, and slanted triangular scheduling. Fine-

tuning employs Unified Focal Loss for extreme class imbalance through asymmetric

weighting and focal modulation. Unlike Wang et al. (2024), who employed SimMIM as

generic computer vision pretext, our denoising approach provides seismic-specific

initialization where learned representations explicitly encode noise-signal discrimination,

directly transferable to distinguishing faults from artifacts. The integration of unfactorized



                                                         S S
three-dimensional global attention with denoising-based pretext training represents a unified



                                                      R E
framework addressing both architectural and training paradigm limitations, enabling

                                                     P
complete volumetric context modeling while providing domain-aligned initialization that

explicitly addresses noise misclassification.

2.1 Datasets and Data Preparation

2.1.1 Pretext Training Datasets for Self-Supervised Denoising

  The model was pretrained on three 3D post-stack seismic surveys from New Zealand's

Taranaki Basin (Kerry-3D, Opunake-3D, Kahu-3D) using a self-supervised denoising
                                   ACCEPTED
                                     ARTICLEMANUSCRIPT
                                             IN PRESS



objective, as described in Mahzad et al. (2026). These volumes exhibit diverse geological

configurations, structural heterogeneity, and noise distributions characteristic of field-

acquired seismic data. Complete details of the surveys, geological context, data

preprocessing, training procedures, and denoising performance are provided in Mahzad et

al. (2026). The resulting pretrained model serves as the backbone for fault segmentation

discriminative transfer learning in this work.

  The self-supervised denoising pretext task follows the training strategy described in

Mahzad et al. (2026). Training pairs were constructed by adding synthetic Gaussian noise n

~ N(0, σ²) to each field-acquired sub-volume, where σ was drawn uniformly between 5% and

30% of the sub-volume's root-mean-square amplitude, yielding a corrupted input ỹ = y + n.

The original field-acquired volume y—which already contains real-world ambient noise—



                                                            S S
served as the reconstruction target, so the network learned to map from a heavily degraded



                                                         R E
version back to its less-corrupted counterpart without requiring paired clean data. This

                                                        P
formulation constitutes a data-driven augmented self-supervised approach: geological


                                                  IN
features such as reflector continuity, fault geometries, and stratigraphic layering exhibit


                                L E
spatial coherence that recurs across surveys, whereas random noise is fundamentally non-


                             IC
                           T
repeating and varies between acquisitions in amplitude distribution, spatial correlation



                        A R
structure, and spectral characteristics. By training simultaneously on three geographically

distinct surveys (Kerry-3D, Opunake-3D, Kahu-3D) with noise corruption at multiple intensity

levels, the network encountered a representative sampling of geological patterns that

generalize across datasets while being exposed to diverse realizations of stochastic noise

that do not. This cross-survey variability acts as an implicit regularizer, enabling the model

to distinguish spatially coherent structures from survey-dependent fluctuations—a capability

that   transfers   directly   to    suppressing   noise-induced   false   positives   during   fault

segmentation. Full training details, loss functions, evaluation metrics, and denoising

performance are reported in Mahzad et al. (2026).

  Due to the quadratic memory scaling of unfactorized global 3D self-attention—O(N²) where

N = D×H×W—full seismic surveys cannot be processed as single volumes. Consistent with
                                    ACCEPTED
                                      ARTICLEMANUSCRIPT
                                              IN PRESS



the pretext training procedure (Mahzad et al., 2026), both pretext training and supervised

fine-tuning decomposed seismic volumes into overlapping 3D sub-volumes of size 64×64×64

voxels with 50% overlap along each spatial axis, yielding a stride of 32 voxels per axis. This

sub-volume size provides sufficient spatial context for global attention to model extended

geological features—including continuous reflectors, fault planes, and stratigraphic

boundaries spanning tens of voxels—while remaining within the memory budget of each RTX

4090 GPU under the AMP and gradient checkpointing strategies described in Section 2.3.4.

During inference, predicted sub-volumes were reassembled into the full survey volume using

Gaussian-weighted blending across overlapping regions. Each voxel's final prediction was

computed as the weighted average of all sub-volume predictions that covered that voxel, with

Gaussian weights attenuating contributions from sub-volume edges where boundary effects



                                                                    S S
are most pronounced. This blending strategy eliminates discontinuities at patch boundaries



                                                                 R E
and suppresses edge artifacts, yielding spatially coherent fault probability maps across the

full survey extent.

  The neural network architecture employed in this study (Figure 6) was originally

developed and trained for seismic denoising (Mahzad et al., 2026), wherein it demonstrated

state-of-the-art performance in suppressing random noise while preserving geological

structures. This same architecture is adapted here for binary fault segmentation via transfer

learning, leveraging the fact that a model pretrained to distinguish noise from coherent

geological features is particularly robust against the well-documented tendency of

segmentation networks to misclassify random noise as faults. The pretrained denoising

model thus serves as pretext training for the downstream fault segmentation task.

  The architecture is a hybrid encoder-decoder network that integrates volumetric

convolutions with true global self-attention in three dimensions. Unlike prior approaches that

rely on factorized attention mechanisms, windowed attention (e.g., Swin transformers), or

2D approximations applied slice-by-slice, this architecture implements full 3D global self-

attention wherein every voxel in a feature volume attends to every other voxel within that

volume. This design decision ensures that both local spatial correlations—captured through

3D convolutional kernels—and long-range dependencies spanning the entire seismic cube

are modeled explicitly and simultaneously at every stage of the network. By combining local

feature extraction via convolution with global contextual reasoning via attention, the model

is equipped to recognize geological patterns that exhibit multi-scale spatial coherence, such

as fault surfaces that may extend across distant regions of a volume.

2.3.2 Transfer Learning Protocol


                        RT
  Following pretext training, the denoising model was adapted to binary fault segmentation

                      A
via a discriminative fine-tuning protocol incorporating three complementary techniques:

layer-wise learning rate decay (LLRD), gradual unfreezing, and slanted triangular learning

rate scheduling (STLR). These methods collectively address the challenge of catastrophic

forgetting—where fine-tuning on a new task degrades pretrained representations—while

enabling effective adaptation to the downstream segmentation objective. The approach is

motivated by the observation that different network depths encode features at varying levels

of abstraction: lower layers capture general-purpose patterns (edges, textures, basic

geological structures) that remain transferable across tasks, whereas higher layers encode

task-specific representations requiring greater adaptation.
                              ACCEPTED
                                ARTICLEMANUSCRIPT
                                        IN PRESS



  Layer-wise learning rate decay assigns differentiated learning rates across network

depths, with higher rates applied to layers closer to the output and progressively lower rates

applied to layers closer to the input. This strategy preserves pretrained low-level features

while allowing high-level representations to adapt more aggressively to the segmentation

task. Following the ULMFiT framework (Howard & Ruder, 2018), model parameters were

partitioned into six hierarchical groups based on architectural depth: output layer (group 0),

decoder blocks (groups 1–2), bottleneck (group 3), and encoder blocks (groups 4–5). Each

group l was assigned a learning rate ηl = ηbase × γ l , where l indexes groups in order of

increasing depth (l = 0 for the output layer, l = 5 for the deepest encoder). Values of ηbase

= 5 × 10 -4 and γ = 0.95 were employed, yielding learning rates that decay exponentially

with depth from 5.00 × 10 -4 (output) to 3.87 × 10 -4 (deepest encoder), resulting in the


                                                         S S
deepest encoder layers being updated approximately 23% slower than the output layer.


                                                      R E
These differentiated learning rates were implemented via the optimizer's parameter groups,

                                                     P
                                              IN
with each group containing parameters from the corresponding architectural level and



                                E
assigned its respective learning rate. During training, the optimizer applies distinct update



                            C L
magnitudes to each group while sharing momentum and adaptive learning statistics across


                         T I
all parameters. This exponential decay ensures that pretrained encoder features—which

capture   fundamental
                      A R geological   patterns   learned   during   denoising—are   modified

conservatively, while decoder features are permitted to adapt rapidly to the new

segmentation objective.

  Rather than fine-tuning all parameters simultaneously, gradual unfreezing progressively

unlocks layers for training in a staged manner, starting from the output layer and proceeding

backward toward the input. This technique prevents abrupt disruption of pretrained features

by allowing the network to first adapt high-level task-specific representations before

permitting modification of low-level general-purpose features. Initially, all encoder and

bottleneck parameters were frozen, rendering only the decoder and output layer trainable.

At predetermined epochs, successive layer groups were unfrozen in top-to-bottom order

(from task-specific to general-purpose features) according to the schedule [0, 10, 20, 30]:

                                ARTICLEMANUSCRIPT
                                        IN PRESS



the output layer and decoder blocks were unfrozen at epoch 0, the bottleneck at epoch 10,

the top encoder blocks at epoch 20, and the deepest encoder blocks at epoch 30. This

incremental adaptation strategy has been demonstrated to preserve pretrained knowledge

more effectively than standard end-to-end fine-tuning, particularly when target datasets are

substantially smaller than pretext training corpora (Howard & Ruder, 2018). By the time

deeper layers are unfrozen, task-specific patterns in higher layers have stabilized, providing

a coherent learning signal that guides refinement of low-level features without catastrophic

interference.

of training via a two-phase profile consisting of a linear warm-up followed by a linear decay.

4 Conclusion

  This study addressed two persistent challenges in seismic fault segmentation: limited

labeled training data and noise misclassification as fault structures. We developed a transfer

learning framework combining hybrid 3D convolutional networks with true global self-

attention—wherein every voxel attends to every other voxel without windowing or



                                                           S
factorization—with multi-survey denoising pretext training and discriminative fine-tuning via



                                                        ES
layer-wise learning rate decay, gradual unfreezing, and slanted triangular scheduling. The


                                                     PR
architecture, originally developed for seismic denoising (Mahzad et al., 2026), was adapted



                                             IN
for binary fault segmentation through transfer learning on the Thebe dataset.

  The precision improvements (8.1% validation, 15.0% test) reflect noise-suppression
